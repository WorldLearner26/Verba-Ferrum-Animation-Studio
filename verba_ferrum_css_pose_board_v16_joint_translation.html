Hierarchy: shoulder translation carries elbow + wrist; elbow translation carries wrist; hip translation carries knee + ankle; knee translation carries ankle. This lets you make strong silhouettes without pulling a hand or foot away from the limb that owns it.
This is the big one: rotation controls pose the skeleton around its pivots; local X/Y controls let you deliberately cheat the 2D silhouette when needed. That gives you much closer control over anticipation, impact, recoil, stepping, crouching, and exaggerated manuscript-style motion.
Parent translation
Move a shoulder or hip and its entire limb follows.
Child translation
Move only the elbow, wrist, knee, or ankle chain when you need finer posing.
Rotation stays independent
Every joint keeps its angle controls alongside X/Y movement.
Whole rig still moves
Global X/Y remains separate from local joint translation.
